About RPM-EE: Purpose & Function

The Recursive Predictive Modeling with Emotional Encoding (RPM-EE) model is designed to simulate how a cognitive agent—human or artificial—processes incoming information, forms predictions about the world, and adapts its future behavior. The model explicitly incorporates the dynamic role of emotion in shaping attention, memory, and decision-making, offering a research tool for understanding adaptive (and maladaptive) cognitive-emotional systems.

What does RPM-EE model? RPM-EE simulates an agent’s inner experience across time, including:

  • Perception and Input: Receiving sensory and contextual data, tagged for salience and emotional significance.
  • Prediction: Generating expectations (what will happen next) recursively, factoring in prior experience, emotional weight, and prediction error.
  • Emotional Encoding: Attaching emotional markers to memories and predictions, dynamically altering what is noticed, rehearsed, or suppressed.
  • Decision and Action: Selecting actions (explore, exploit, stabilize, etc.) based on a competition between plausible future scenarios, weighted by both plausibility and emotional relevance.
  • Adaptation and Replay: Learning from outcomes, pruning outdated models, and replaying emotionally charged events for further analysis or integration.

What is RPM-EE meant to do?
The goal of RPM-EE is to provide a laboratory for exploring how emotion, memory, and recursive prediction interact in both healthy and disrupted cognition. It can be used to:

  • Model decision-making under uncertainty and stress
  • Study the mechanisms behind rumination, overthinking, and maladaptive replay
  • Test hypotheses about executive dysfunction, emotional override, and simulation error in neurodivergent conditions (e.g., ASD, ADHD, OCD)
  • Generate data for research, clinical insight, or theoretical exploration in computational neuropsychology

Why use a simulation?
RPM-EE enables the safe, rapid, and repeatable testing of cognitive-emotional theories that would be impractical or unethical to examine in humans. By manipulating variables and replaying scenarios, researchers and clinicians can probe how small changes in perception, emotion, or memory weighting may cascade into dramatically different behaviors or internal states.

What makes RPM-EE unique?
Unlike classic models, RPM-EE integrates recursive prediction and emotionally driven selection at every step. Its modular architecture allows for the simulation of complex internal phenomena—such as emotional fatigue, pattern recognition, memory compression, and prediction error—at a granular level. The system is designed for extensibility, supporting batch simulations and live interface analysis.
