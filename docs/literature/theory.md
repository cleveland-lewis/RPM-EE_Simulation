# Theorizing Recursive Predictive Modeling with Emotional Encoding (RPM-EE)
---
## Introduction

RPM-EE aims to integrate established areas of cognitive neuroscience and explore how their interactions give rise to emergent properties. The human experience depends heavily on emergent qualities—attributes that arise from complex systems and cannot be fully explained within a single domain. These include consciousness, self-awareness, subjective experience (qualia), intentionality, and a sense of agency. Such properties result from intricate interactions among predictive processing, emotional valence, and recursive self-modeling, but cannot be reduced to any single neural or computational process. In RPM-EE, observable emergent qualities encompass human emotions, self-concept, symbolism, and metacognition. Given their complexity, RPM-EE relies on large-scale probabilistic analyses, requiring repeated, random trials over extended periods. Without significant computing power, advanced algorithms for modeling neural activity, and a statistically meaningful human sample, modeling an individual accurately demands substantial abstraction. Over multiple trials, this abstraction represents broader human populations rather than individuals. While current technology can technically model a single person, a minimal sample size (*n=1*) would require major algorithmic adjustments, and the high number of trials necessary would make such efforts prohibitively resource-intensive, limiting the practicality of large-scale research or broad access.

## Methods
The RPM-EE model utilizes the Central Limit Theorem (CLT), which states that the as with an appropriate number of entities, the distribution of the sample approaches a normal curve, meaning that the mean, mode and the median all align in the center of the graph—a bell curve. Given that this model plans to take in account as much variance as possible through randomization and inclusion of extremes, it is taken as an assumption that this data which this model is fundamentally based upon is normally distributed. However, it is important to remember that this is still an assumption thus meaning that the rest of the model is therefore built upon assumptions, albeit and a statistically significant assumption, with as significant assertions.
